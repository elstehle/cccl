### T?: a stock cub::BlockRadixSort pass at 256 threads costs about 740 + 82 x IPT cycles on 32-bit words and 815 + 100 x IPT on 64-bit words, so a full block sort is 2.1x to 6.5x a radix select at k = N/2 and never a selection primitive
applies to: B6, W4's chunk merges, the sorted-output pass (phase 3b), any block primitive that considers a full sort of the tile (cub::BlockRadixSort directly, and cub::BlockMergeSort by the July 2026 hybrid sort numbers), block scope at 256 threads, 16-bit keys in a 32-bit word and 32-bit keys in a 64-bit word, N 1024 to 8192

evidence: SASS of the bf16 N 2048 latency kernel (two inlined sort calls plus the harness): 26 BAR.SYNC, 15 SHFL.UP, 56 LDS.U16, 24 STS.U16, 96 STS, 47 LDS, 0 ATOMS, 0 STL, 0 LDL, 1008 instructions, the pass loop a runtime while over begin_bit (BRA.U). fp32: 32 STS.64, 16 LDS.64, 16 SHF.R.U64, 888 instructions. Registers 48, 56, 79, 128 (bf16) and 48, 64, 112, 206 (fp32) at IPT 4 to 32, no spill. The fixed part per pass is six barriers plus the 256-thread BlockScan over 9 packed counters per thread (about 2.5 times the one-scan emission of T29). The per-item part is shared-memory wavefronts over 8 warps: per item and pass one LDS.U16 and one STS.U16 on the thread's digit counter (a dependent chain through aliasing pointers), one LDS.U16 for the rank, one scatter STS to a random rank with 3 to 4 way bank conflicts, one gather LDS, about 4 to 5 wavefronts x 8 warps x IPT with the conflicts doubling the scatter, which is the 82 to 100 cycles per item. At IPT 32 the per-item part is 2600 of 3376 cycles per pass.
how to use: Do not sort to select. A block radix select pays 2 to 4 histogram sweeps without moving data and one emission, a block radix sort pays 4 to 8 ranking sweeps each followed by a full exchange of every item, and the gap does not close at any k up to N/2 or at any N in the block range. Use the fit to budget a sorted output: a full sort of N items costs passes x (740 + 82 x N / 256) cycles for 32-bit words at 256 threads, a post-sort of k selected words costs the same formula at N = k, so at k = N/2 the full sort is cheaper than select plus post-sort for 16-bit keys (5550 against 2550 + 4279 at N 2048) and at k at most N/4 the post-sort route is cheaper, while for fp32 (64-bit words, 8 passes) the post-sort route wins at every k. A sort that must be cheaper needs a ranking without the per-item counter chain and an exchange without the random scatter, not more threads or wider digits (the fixed part is already 2.5 scans).
caveats: One node, one toolkit, the stock 4-bit digit and the default scan algorithm (RadixBits 5 to 8 not measured, the raking segment grows with the digit width), random normal input (the scatter conflicts are data dependent, a sorted or tie-heavy input conflicts differently), latency regime at one block (the throughput regime loses the same factor, the wavefronts are SM-wide). 512 threads at N 8192 would halve the per-item part and add 16 cycles per barrier (about 1.5x on the sort, not measured, cannot reach the select). The hybrid block sort study of July 2026 (cccl-exp-perf, HYBRID_BLOCK_SORT_RESULTS.md, B200, CUDA 13.1) has the stock BlockMergeSort at 11684 cycles for 2048 fp32 keys and 14795 for pairs, the same band.
