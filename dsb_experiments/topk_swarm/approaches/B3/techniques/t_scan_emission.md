evidence: approaches/B3/block_topk_compact.cuh `emit` and `compact`: per thread two counts (items strictly above the pivot prefix, items equal to it) packed as (above << 16) | ties into one 32-bit word, one cub::BlockScan ExclusiveSum, then one predicated STS per selected item at out[base + prefix]. B1's epilogue: one atomicAdd_block with return per selected item on one of two counters (T9: 55 cycles uncontended, serialized under contention), key scatter and gather, value scatter and gather, three barriers.
how to use: For index-only output never assign output positions by shared atomic tickets with return. Count the two classes per thread in blocked order, scan one packed word (16-bit fields hold counts up to 32768), and store each selected index at its prefix position. The cost is one block scan (about 300 cycles with one barrier) plus one predicated store per selected item, independent of k, and the positions are in index order, which makes prefer_smaller_index free: the ties at the k-th key are ranked by the same scan and the first k_remaining in index order are taken (prefer_larger_index by reversing the rank). B3 measured prefer_smaller_index within 0.5 percent of not_guaranteed in every cell.
caveats: Needs the k-th key's prefix and the tie count from the last radix pass (both are in the pass state). At k below 64 the ticket epilogue is within a few percent (B1's atomics are few), the gain starts near k 256 and reaches 1.6x to 1.7x at k = N/16. The packed word limits N to 32768 per 16-bit field, use two scans or 64-bit above that.
