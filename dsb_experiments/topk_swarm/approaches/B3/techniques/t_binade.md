### T?: an 8-bit radix digit on float keys pairs two binades, the first-pass pivot bucket holds 29 percent of N at k/N between 1/16 and 1/8 on normal data, a 9-bit first digit caps it at 14 percent
applies to: B1, B3, B4, N6, W3 and every radix select on fp32 or bf16 keys, any scope
measured: host model approaches/B3/model.py (`--stats`, 16 random normal inputs per cell, the harness's random pattern): pivot bucket of the first pass as a fraction of N, 8-bit digit 0.286 to 0.289 mean (max 0.31) at k = N/16 for N 1024 to 32768 both types, 0.022 to 0.024 at k/N <= 1/64, 9-bit digit 0.135 to 0.137 (max 0.17) and 0.022, 10-bit digit 0.044 to 0.093. On the GPU (B200 umbriel-b200-047 sm_100 nvcc 13.4.92 2026-10-25, harness latency chain slope) the 9-bit first digit against the 8-bit one in the same B3 code: N 32768 k 2048 1.268x (bf16) and 1.283x (fp32), N 16384 k 1024 1.149x and 1.238x, N 8192 k 1024 1.036x and 1.181x, where the 9-bit digit lets the k = N/16 cells compact 14 percent of the tile instead of 29 percent, and 0.90x to 0.95x at N 1024 to 4096 small k where the 512-bin scan and zeroing cost more than the smaller bucket saves. File state/results/opt-B3/B3_digit9_vs_B3_straight_b3_dig4_20261025_133045.json.
evidence: the top 8 bits of a twiddled fp32 or bf16 key are the sign and the seven high exponent bits, so buckets 2e and 2e+1 of the exponent share one digit: [0.5, 1) and [1, 2) fall in one bucket (15 plus 13.6 percent of a unit normal), [2, 4) and [4, 8) in the next (2.3 percent). The k-th largest of N normals lies in [1, 2) for k/N from about 1/40 to 1/6 and in [0.5, 1) up to about 1/3, the band of the chunk merges, H2O and SnapKV in ideas/WORKLOADS.md.
how to use: When the first radix pass decides how many candidates survive (compaction, candidate buffers, early exit), read 9 bits in the first pass (sign plus the whole exponent, 512 bins) and 8 bits afterwards, and scan only the live bins of each pass. When the first pass only narrows a prefix and every pass sweeps the whole tile (plain AIR), the digit width does not change the pass count on random floats and 8 bits stays cheaper (R2). N6's equi-width bins avoid the question by construction.
caveats: Distribution specific: on uniform random bits every 8-bit bucket holds N/256. Normal logits are the harness pattern and the common LLM case. The 512-bin pass costs about 100 cycles more than a 256-bin pass at 256 threads (two bins per thread in the scan, two zeroing stores) in this code, which is the whole loss at N 1024.
