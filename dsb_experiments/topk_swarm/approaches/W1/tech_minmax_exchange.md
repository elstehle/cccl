evidence: cuobjdump -sass of the N 128 k 32 latency kernel (two network copies, 150 SHFL in both): min max form 632 instructions with 306 VIMNMX.U32 (a predicated max and a predicated min per shuffle stage), 24 ISETP, 7 SEL. Compare and select form 792 instructions with 281 ISETP, 157 SEL, 80 LOP3, 6 VIMNMX. No BSSY, BRA 5 (the harness) on both, no STL or LDL. The dependent chain after the shuffle is one 6-cycle VIMNMX against ISETP, a second ISETP with the first as predicate input, then SEL. approaches/W1/w1x_sass_b200.txt and w1x_sass_gb200.txt hold the histograms of all kernels per cell.
how to use: on an unsigned word whose integer order is the selection order, write the shuffle-stage exchange as k = keep_max ? max(k, other) : min(k, other) and the in-register exchange as lo = min(a, b), hi = max(a, b) (approaches/W1/w1_common.cuh, word_ops<uint32_t>). ptxas emits the two predicated VIMNMX itself, no inline PTX. Do not write it as if ((a > b || b > a) && dir == (a > b)) a = b (the cub::detail::WarpBitonicSortNetwork form) or as a = (b > a) != keep_min ? b : a (the W1b form), both compile to compare and select chains. A bool comparator object hides the min max form from the compiler, so a generic network that wants this codegen needs a min max customization point next to the comparator.
caveats: 32-bit unsigned words only. The direction predicate must stay a cheap lane function ((lane & Stride) != 0 xor a compile-time or lane-bit direction), otherwise its ISETP joins the chain. One toolkit (13.4.92), two hosts. The code structure around the exchange adds its own factor: the same compare and select exchange in template recursion against cub::detail::WarpBitonicTopK itself is 1.03x to 1.41x faster on B200 and 1.3x to 1.7x on an aarch64 build (card W1x_codegen findings), so the exchange alone does not reproduce the whole W1_level against W1_packed gap.
