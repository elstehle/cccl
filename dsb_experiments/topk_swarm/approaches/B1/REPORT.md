## 1. What it is

`block_topk_air` selects the k largest of a register tile by radix selection: 8-bit digits of the order-preserving key image, most significant first, one shared-memory atomic histogram over the surviving candidates per pass, a fused block scan that finds the bucket holding the k-th key, early exit when that bucket holds exactly the remaining k, and a partition stage that scatters the selected items by shared atomic tickets. The main header (B1_reference) does this on blocked tiles with a pairs exchange epilogue. The striped branch (B1_striped, the baseline of this report) does it on striped tiles.

The variants of this report keep the pass machinery of the striped branch and change what is around it:

- `B1_idx`: index-only epilogue. Selected items write their striped source index (i x 256 + tid, computed) to the caller's k-slot output by ticket. No exchange buffer, no gather, no memoized key copy, raw shift-and-mask digit extractor, no -0.0 normalization. Temp storage 2128 bytes at every N.
- `B1_idxw`: `B1_idx` with warp-aggregated tickets (ballot per slot, one atomic per warp and slot). A measured negative.
- `B1_nz`: `B1_idx` plus the prologue -0.0 to +0.0 normalization, to price the tie-class semantics.
- `B1_det`: `B1_idx` under `prefer_smaller_index`: normalization plus the warp-redundant rank over the exact ties (knowledge base 4.5 approach C), index order for free on striped tiles, taken ties written at total_selected + rank without an atomic.
- `B1_n6` and `B1_n6b`: the N6 idea (`ideas/queue/N6_equiwidth_bucket_select.md`) built on the same pass machinery, see section 5.

## 2. Contract

Input: a full striped tile of N = 256 x IPT keys in registers (thread t holds items t + i x 256), fp32 or bf16, k in [1, N) at run time, select the largest. Output: k source indices in the caller's shared array, unspecified order (ticket order). Temp storage: histogram double buffer, scan storage, pass state and two counters (2128 bytes), plus 128 bytes for the tie table under `prefer_smaller_index`, plus 4 KB of per-bucket trackers and bracket partials for N6 (6352 bytes). Synchronization: `__syncthreads` inside (2 per pass plus 1 init, plus 1 in the tie phase when it runs), the caller separates consecutive calls that reuse the temp storage by a barrier. The input keys are clobbered (twiddled in place) by the radix variants and left intact by N6. NaN: the radix variants twiddle it to the largest key (never produced by focus-set inputs), N6 does not support it. Signed zero: `not_guaranteed` variants keep -0.0 below +0.0 in the key order (any valid set, the oracle compares keys with ==), `prefer_smaller_index` variants fold -0.0 onto +0.0 once in the prologue so both zeros are one tie class. `prefer_smaller_index` is supported up to IPT 64 (the tie decision is a 64-bit mask per thread). Partial tiles and the blocked arrangement are not in these prototypes (the striped header keeps both).

### 4.1 The N6 kill experiment

Explore item 1 (host simulation, `sim_passes.md` section 2, 200 rows per cell, numpy's normal generator): with the thread-max bracket the pivot bin after one 256-bin pass holds on average 1.1 to 7.3 items (p90 at most 11, max 16) in every random cell with k at most 256 at N 1024, 4096 and 32768, for both key types, so 100 percent of the rows finish with a warp-sized finish or less. The exact exit (pivot count equals the remaining k, or one distinct value in the pivot bin) fires after pass 1 in 11 to 94 percent of the rows and after pass 2 in 97 to 100 percent. For bf16 at N 4096 and 32768 the pivot bin is a single bf16 value in 100 percent of the rows (d1 1.0), so a representable-value test would end bf16 in one pass there. tie_heavy and relu_quantized rows end in one pass as one tie class. A single outlier (1e30) collapses the bracket (pivot bin 800 to 1500 items), which the implementation handles by falling back to key passes after a first pass that does not shrink the candidates to a quarter of the tile. For k above 256 the full bracket leaves 37 to 217 items in the pivot bin at k 1024 and 4096 (second pass needed, exact after it in 68 to 100 percent).

Explore item 2 (B1 with the bin function swapped in pass 1 at N 1024 k 64 bf16 against B1, same session): the swap is the whole N6 prototype on the AIR pass machinery (the image is radix-selected with the same histogram, scan and state code), measured against B1_idx in batch n6b and against itself in n6c. The per-item cost of the bin function is about 4 instructions per item per pass over the radix extractor (FADD, FFMA, two IMNMX against a shift and a mask), hidden in the dependency-bound pass at IPT 4 and about 7 percent of the sweep at IPT 128, within the card's 10 percent. At the named cell (N 1024 k 64 bf16) the first N6 build measured 2213 against 1877 (the tie class detected one pass late) and the per-bucket build 2039 against the first build's 2234 in the next batch, so N6 is still about 8 percent behind the radix index-only path at that cell, 6 to 9 percent ahead at k 16 and ahead at N 4096 for fp32.

Verdict: not killed. Both criteria of the card hold (one pass plus a finish in 100 percent of the random rows at k at most 256, per-item cost under 10 percent), the measured gains at k 16 and at fp32 N 4096 are real, and the measured losses (bf16 at k 64 to 256 at N 1024, everything at N 32768) are implementation: the two-pass cost per pass is higher than radix's (bracket, image, tracking) and the IPT 128 kernel spills. N6 needs its own optimizer or a second session of this one for the register problem, the pass-1 representable-value exit for bf16, and a tighter lower bound for k above 256.

## 5. N6 as built

`approaches/B1/block_topk_n6.cuh`. Prologue: bf16 widened to fp32, per-thread max and min, three `redux.sync` per warp (max of maxima, min of maxima, min of minima), lane 0 stores, one barrier shared with the histogram zeroing, every thread reduces the 8 partials. Bracket [lo, hi] with hi the tile max and lo the min over threads of the per-thread max for k at most 256 (256 items are at or above it) or the tile min for larger k, both clamped to finite values. Scale (2^23 - 8) / (hi - lo), finite and nonzero in every case (an overflowed or empty width takes a fixed scale and the degenerate pass falls back). Image per item per pass: `bits(fmaf(v - lo, scale, 2^23))` clamped into [bits(2^23), bits(2^23) + 2^23 - 1] (values below lo and -inf on the bottom, +inf on the top), monotone in v. Passes over 8, 8 and 7 bits of the image on AIR's machinery, exits: pivot count equals the remaining k, or (from the second pass on) the pivot bucket's tracked key min equals its max (one tie class). Fallbacks: after a first pass that leaves more than a quarter of the tile as candidates, or after the three image passes with distinct candidates, radix passes on the key bits restricted to the candidates (image prefix and key prefix), starting at the byte of the highest differing bit of the tracked min and max (or the top byte on the early fallback), 2 bytes for bf16 and 4 for fp32. Epilogue as B1_idx with the item class from the image prefix (and the key prefix after a fallback), tie-break as B1_det under `prefer_smaller_index`. The oracle battery (tie_heavy, sorted, pivot_tie, all_equal, signed_zero, inf_denorm, quantized) passes on all 22 cells of both N6 variants.

## 6. Ideas not yet tried

- The det fix: the opaque counter index is in `block_topk_b1.cuh` and `block_topk_n6.cuh` (deterministic paths) and needs one batch against B1_idx under `not_guaranteed`. Expected: the 344-cycle structural cost vanishes and `prefer_smaller_index` costs the tie phase alone (about 70 cycles at IPT 4 on bf16 rows with ties, nothing on fp32 rows without), plus the 1.5 to 6 percent normalization.
- Digit schedule by IPT for fp32: 11+11+10 or 12+12+8 at IPT 64 and 128 where the host simulation saves 0.3 to 0.9 passes per call and the sweep dominates the pass. The scan cost of wider digits is measured at IPT 4 only (R2). One batch at N 16384 and 32768.
- N6 register problem at IPT 128: find what holds 128 extra words across the passes (ptxas -v of the n6c against the idx1 build in the gen directories), bound it with a two-half item loop or by dropping the fp32 copy for bf16 (recompute from the bf16 bits).
- N6 pass-1 representable-value exit for bf16: after the scan, compute the pivot bin's value interval conservatively and test whether it contains at most one bf16 value, block-uniform, no per-item cost. The simulation says this ends bf16 in one pass at N 4096 and 32768 for every k at most 256.
- N6 lower bound for k above 256: the min over threads of the per-thread m-th largest (B4's bound, m = ceil(k / 256)), one small per-thread network, extends the one-pass regime to the chunk-merge shapes (k 1024 to 4096).
- A FINISH for pivot bins of at most 32 candidates (compact, one warp selects by all-pairs rank): saves the second pass structure (about 450 cycles at IPT 4) in the cases where the exact exit does not fire after pass 1, not written.
- IPT above 64 under `prefer_smaller_index` (a second tie-mask word, or the taken bit recomputed from the rank in the epilogue).
- The pairs output form on the index-only epilogue (scatter 8-byte pairs by the same ticket) for the full evaluation.
- An `sm_100f` variant has no lever here: the bracket reductions are u32 `redux.sync` on twiddled keys (T2 says the f32 form saves 18 cycles per round, one round per call).

## 7. Determinism and ordering support

- Set determinism: the radix selection is set-deterministic by construction up to the ties at the k-th key. `not_guaranteed` variants take the ties in atomic ticket order (not reproducible).
- `prefer_smaller_index`: B1_det and the N6 variants under that requirement take the ties with the smallest source indices by the warp-redundant rank (per warp and tile slot a ballot of the candidates into a table, one barrier, every warp derives the bases itself by a shuffle scan, rank below the remaining k is taken, position total_selected + rank without an atomic). On striped tiles slot-major thread order is index order, so no index compare is needed. Correct on every battery pattern including signed_zero (exact index set with 2k zeros of both signs). Measured cost in this build: plus 19 to 23 percent latency at N 1024 k 16 (2138 against 1794 fp32, 2177 against 1767 bf16), plus 24 to 51 percent at N 4096 (fp32 k 64 5684 against 4588, bf16 k 64 5801 against 3839), minus 17 to 38 percent throughput, of which about 344 cycles per call are the compiler's warp aggregation of the ticket atomic (T23, fix implemented and unmeasured), 25 to 31 cycles the -0.0 normalization (B1_nz) and about 70 cycles at IPT 4 the tie phase itself on bf16 rows with ties. The knowledge base 4.5 figure of about plus 1 percent on random data stands for the tie phase, not for this epilogue spelling.
- `prefer_larger_index`: not built, the rank test flips to the complement (skip the first candidates - k_remaining).
- Sorted output: not inherent, a post-sort of k.
- Signed zero: see the contract. NaN: unsupported in N6, largest key in the radix variants.
