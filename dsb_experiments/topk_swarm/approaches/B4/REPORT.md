## 1. What it is

The minimum over the threads of the per-thread maximum is an exact lower bound on the k-th largest key for every k up to the thread count: the thread maxima are k or more distinct items at or above it. It costs one max per item, two `redux.sync` per warp and the barrier the histogram zeroing needs anyway (the N6 bracket, T25). On random input about 1500 items of the tile are at or above it at every N from 4096 on (31 percent of N 4096, 4.7 percent of N 32768, host simulation `sim_bound.md` section 1 and the GPU batches agree), so B4 replaces the first histogram pass of a radix or bucket selection, which issues one shared atomic per item of the tile, by a compare sweep that compacts the candidates into shared memory in thread order, and runs the selection over a few candidates per thread: one pass over the fixed-point image of the candidates (N6's digit over the bracket, 256 bins) leaves a pivot bin of 2 to 24 items for k 16 to 256, which one warp resolves exactly by redux rounds on (key, inverted index) words (the W2 round, T3 and T19, one redux per round for bf16, two for fp32), while the items above the pivot bin are emitted by position. For k above the thread count the per-thread items are split into m groups and the bound is the minimum over all m x 256 group maxima, accepted at run time only when the candidate count reaches k. When the candidate count exceeds the buffer (tie floods, all-equal input, a blocked-sorted tile) or falls short of k, the same pass machinery runs over the register tile on the key bytes (the exact fallback).

Exactness, in this order after every pass: the pivot set is exactly the remaining k (emit all), the pivot set has at most 32 items (warp finish, exact under both requirements), the pivot set is a tie class (its block min equals its max: any k_rem of it under `not_guaranteed`, index passes under `prefer_smaller_index`), else the next digit (image digits 8, 8, 7 bits, then key bytes from the highest differing byte of the pivot set's min and max, then the inverted index bits, which end with a single item).

Four builds of the same algorithm were measured, each against the previous one: `B4_base` (the design as written), `B4_v2` (per-slot structure fixed after a phase profile), `B4_v3` (sweeps in the value domain, single-pass ticket emission), `B4_v4` (branch-free atomics in the pass and the emission). Section 4 has the chain, section 3 the profile that drove it.

## 2. Contract

Input: a full striped tile of N = 256 x IPT keys in registers (thread t holds items t + i x 256), fp32 or bf16, k in [1, N) at run time with a compile-time hint for the group count and the buffer size (the adapter derives both from K), select the largest. Output: k source indices in the caller's shared array, unspecified order. Requirements `not_guaranteed` and `prefer_smaller_index` in one code path (the tie-class exit differs, see section 6). Temp storage: two 256-bin histograms, the bin prefix, the scan storage, bracket and reduction partials, the dummy words (v4), a 32-word pivot buffer, and the candidate buffer of 4096 words (bf16, key and inverted index in one word) or 4096 pairs (fp32) for k up to 256, 8192 up to k 1024, 16384 above, never more than N: 22.9 KB (bf16) and 39.3 KB (fp32) at k up to 256 and N 32768 (B1_idx 2.1 KB, B3_tuned 69.7 KB). Synchronization: `__syncthreads` inside (bracket, count scan, compaction, three per finishing pass, one before the warp finish, one more in the multi-pass emission and in the lazy tie test), the caller separates consecutive calls that reuse the temp storage by a barrier. N up to 32768 (16-bit count fields), 128 and 512 threads accepted by the adapter and untested. NaN: a NaN with the sign bit clear orders above +inf and is selected first, one with the sign bit set orders below -inf, the all-ones pattern collides with the empty-slot sentinel of the warp finish (unsupported), never produced by focus-set inputs. Signed zero: equal in the value-domain sweeps of v3 and v4, folded onto +0.0 when the candidates are twiddled under `prefer_smaller_index` (one tie class), kept distinct in the key order under `not_guaranteed` (any valid set, the oracle compares keys with ==). The input keys are left intact.

## 3. Where the time goes

The harness measures whole calls. To attribute cycles the primitive carries clock64 stamps between its phases behind a macro that compiles to nothing in the harness builds (`B4_PROFILE`, `profile_phases.cu`, thread 0 writes the stamps, median over 32 calls on one block, same node). The stamps add about 15 percent to the harness numbers, the phase shares are what matters.

| phase, cycles | base N 4096 bf16 k 16 | v2 | v3 | base N 32768 fp32 k 256 | v2 | v3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prologue and bracket (widen or twiddle, thread max, redux, barrier) | 412 | 420 | 360 | 1366 | 1371 | 462 |
| candidate count sweep and block scan | 557 | 669 | 758 | 1577 | 1747 | 2472 |
| compaction store sweep and barrier | 294 | 470 | 265 | 1771 | 3078 | 1892 |
| candidate loads (16 slots) and image | 226 | 717 | 976 | 212 | 805 | 692 |
| first pass (histogram, scan, state) | 1708 | 898 | 920 | 1753 | 950 | 989 |
| emission (classify, scan, stores, or tickets) | 3863 | 2027 | 1108 | 4260 | 2729 | 1792 |
| warp finish | about 150 | about 150 | about 150 | about 300 | about 300 | about 300 |
| total | 7199 | 5306 | 4452 | 11495 | 11153 | 8904 |

Three facts of this table decided the iterations.

1. In the base build every candidate slot was its own uniform branch (`if (j < slots)`) around a body of 36 SASS instructions (the image recomputed, a three-part prefix test, three atomics), so the finish ran as 16 dependent chains back to back whether the slot was live or not, about 240 cycles per slot (T56). Grouping the slots by four, computing the image once per candidate at load time and dropping the min and max tracking atomics halved the pass and the emission (v2).
2. The three register sweeps at IPT 128 cost 4700 to 6200 cycles in every build: about 10 cycles per item slot for 7 to 10 instructions per item, an issue rate of about one instruction per four cycles per warp at two warps per scheduler, half of the nominal. The twiddle sweep of base and v2 (1400 to 1800 cycles) is gone in v3 (the sweeps compare fp32 values and store raw bits, the candidates are twiddled when loaded), but the compaction store sweep issues 128 predicated shared stores per thread and the count sweep two instructions per item, so the floor of the sweeps is about 3300 cycles at IPT 128 with this instruction count. Fewer instructions per item (packed bf16 compares, T18) is the only lever left there.
3. After v2 the structure, not the slot work, was the cost: four block scans at 350 to 430 cycles each, nine barriers, and a divergent branch per slot around the candidate loads (700 cycles for 16 loads, each slot waiting for the previous one's load latency) and around the emission tickets (1100 to 1800 cycles in v3). v3 removed the emission scan and its classify sweep by taking each selected item's position from the exclusive bin prefix of the pass plus a per-bin ticket, v4 made the tickets and the pass atomics branch-free (every slot issues its atomic, to a private dummy word when it takes no part, T32's proposal) and loads the candidates per uniform group.

Registers: 61 to 64 at IPT 4, 125 to 128 at IPT 16, 128 to 153 at IPT 32, 254 to 255 from IPT 64 (B1_idx 32, 47, 64, 250), spill 1.4 to 1.6 KB at IPT 128 in v3 and 2.5 KB under `prefer_smaller_index` (the index passes are a third pass body over the tile in the fallback). The 255 registers at IPT 64 (B1_idx 64) are the tile plus the 16 candidate slots times three words plus the fallback's state; throughput at N 16384 runs at one block per SM against B1_idx's two.

### 4.1 B4_v4 and the final pairing

v4 made the pass histogram and the emission tickets branch-free (every slot issues its atomic, the slots that take no part hit a private dummy word) and loads the candidates per uniform group of four. Against v3 (batch b7) it is a wash within 3 percent at N 4096 and above (bf16 N 16384 k 64 5191 against 5602 is the best cell, fp32 N 32768 k 256 6962 against 6728 the worst), 1.1x to 1.2x at k 2048 from the grouped loads, and 0.92x to 0.96x at N 1024, where sixteen tickets with return serve four live slots. The profile says why: the branch-free pass saves 200 to 270 cycles, the branch-free emission costs 170 to 270 more (sixteen atomicAdd with return per thread, ten of them to dummy words, and the returned value on the critical path of the store). T32's dummy-word proposal therefore pays for fire-and-forget histogram increments and not for tickets with return.

The final pairing (batch b8, v4 against B3_tuned in one lock section, cycles per call, bf16 then fp32, `not_guaranteed`): N 32768 k 16 7346 and 6256 against 19130 and 18604 (2.60x and 2.97x), k 64 7800 and 6689 against 19129 and 19454 (2.45x and 2.91x), k 256 8087 and 6969 against 19139 and 20021 (2.37x and 2.87x), k 1024 11940 and 10491 against 19235 and 20409 (1.61x and 1.95x), k 2048 26016 and 25802 against 19236 and 20782 (0.74x and 0.81x); N 16384 k 64 5190 and 4666 against 11004 and 10459 (2.12x and 2.24x), k 256 5594 and 5096 against 11019 and 11352; N 8192 k 64 4639 and 4291 against 6260 and 6092 (1.35x and 1.42x); N 4096 k 64 4240 and 4340 against 3797 and 4143 (0.90x and 0.95x); N 1024 k 16 2534 and 2457 against 1885 and 2034. `prefer_smaller_index` at N 32768 k 64: 8594 and 6723 against 19113 and 19520 (2.22x and 2.90x). Throughput at N 32768 832 and 941 Gelem per s against 400 and 345 (2.1x and 2.7x), at N 16384 669 and 738 against 465 and 312, at N 8192 646 and 693 against 459 and 398, at N 4096 414 and 417 against 543 and 459 (0.76x and 0.91x). ## 5. The host simulation (task 1, `sim_bound.py`, `sim_bound.md`)

300 rows per cell (100 at N 32768), the harness patterns, striped and blocked arrangement, N 1024 to 32768, k 16 to 2048, bounds m1 (min of thread maxima), gm (min of m = ceil(k / 256) group maxima, a priori valid), gmh (m = ceil(k / 512), valid when the candidate count reaches k) and topm (exact per-thread m-th largest).

- Candidates of the m1 bound (k at most 256): mean 795 at N 1024 (78 percent), 1300 at N 4096 (31 percent), 1430 at N 8192 (17), 1500 at N 16384 (9), 1530 at N 32768 (4.7); p99 1900 to 2700, maximum 2100 to 3400; the count is set by the 256 maxima and hardly depends on N. A buffer of 4096 never overflowed on random, quantized or outlier rows; 2048 overflows 0.3 to 10 percent of the rows. Blocked and striped agree on random input.
- Sorted input: striped tiles compact to exactly 256 candidates at every N (the thread maxima are the last row), blocked tiles to N minus IPT plus 1 (fallback). The striped arrangement is a requirement, not a preference.
- Tie floods (tie_heavy, pivot_tie, all_equal) and relu_quantized at k 1024 and above put the whole tile at or above the bound (fallback), quantized_random and one_outlier behave like random.
- k above 256: gm with m = 4 leaves 21 percent of N 32768 (6900 items, 27 slots), gmh with m = 2 leaves 3300 (13 slots, never short of k in 300 rows, no overflow of 8192), topm 2950 but costs a per-thread partial sort of the tile. The adapter uses gmh with a run-time check.
- First finishing pass over the candidates: the float image (N6's) leaves a pivot bin of 1.5 to 3 items at k 16, 3 to 4 at k 64, 7 to 12 at k 256, never above 32 on random rows with k at most 256 (24 at most), 28 to 62 at k 1024 to 2048 with the gm bound (a second pass in half of the rows), one bf16 value per bin from N 4096 on. An integer offset digit on the twiddled key (d = tk minus lo, 256 equal integer bins) is as selective only when the bracket is narrow and collapses to 1200 items whenever the bracket spans the sign (every k above 256 at N 8192, 0.4 percent of the rows at N 4096, every row at N 1024), which is why the finish uses the float image.

## 6. Determinism and ordering support

Set-deterministic and index-deterministic by construction in every build: the items above the pivot set are an exact set, the pivot set is resolved by the warp finish (key descending, index ascending), by the tie-class rule, or by index passes. `prefer_smaller_index` is the same code path with two differences, the fold of -0.0 onto +0.0 when the candidates are twiddled, and the index passes instead of "any k_rem" for a tie class larger than the warp finish. Measured cost 4 to 17 percent (section 4), all of it register pressure from the third pass body. `prefer_larger_index` is one flipped comparison in the warp finish and the index digit, not built. Output order: unspecified (bin order then ticket order in the single-pass emission, scan order in the multi-pass emission, the warp finish's winners in rank order after the items above). Sorted output: not inherent, a post-sort of k.

## 7. Ideas not yet tried

1. Packed bf16 sweeps on the `packed_striped` arrangement (two keys per word): HMNMX2 maxima and HSET2 compares halve the count and max sweeps and hold 128 keys in 64 registers (T18), predicted 1000 cycles at N 32768 and two blocks per SM at IPT 64.
2. The fallback out of the fast path's function: a `__noinline__` tile fallback, or a fallback that re-reads the keys from the caller's memory, would take the index-pass and key-pass bodies out of the register allocation of the fast path (255 registers at IPT 64, 1.4 to 2.5 KB of spill at IPT 128).
3. A custom block scan (warp shuffle scan, warp totals in shared memory, one barrier, about 230 cycles) for the count scan and the pass scan in place of cub's BlockScan (350 to 430 cycles here).
4. The per-thread group count for k above 256 as the exact per-thread m-th largest when IPT is small (the sort is cheap at IPT 16), gmh with the run-time check above.
5. A smaller candidate buffer (2048 words, 8 slots) with the fallback at 0.3 to 10 percent of the rows, against the 16-slot pass and emission cost.
6. 128 and 512 threads (accepted by the adapter, unmeasured): at 128 threads the bound is the 128th order statistic (twice the candidates per thread) and the barriers are cheaper (T11).
7. The W2 finish on a 64-word buffer (list depth 2) for k 1024 to 2048, where the pivot bin holds 28 to 62 items after the first pass and a second pass runs in half of the rows.
8. N6 and B4 as one primitive: N6's first pass over the tile for IPT at most 16 (B4's compaction does not pay at N 1024), B4's compaction above, the warp finish in both.
